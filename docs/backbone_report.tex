\title{\textbf{SESiL-foundation: an SSL backbone as the population's shared
origin}\\
\large Implementation, verification, and the budget-allocation study}
\author{}
\date{2026-08-25}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This report documents the unsupervised-pretraining stage added to SESiL: a
SimSiam backbone trained on unlabelled CIFAR-10 and handed to every expert as a
shared starting point, replacing independent per-expert supervised pretraining.
It covers what was built, how the handoff was verified, what the budget study
found, and one reproducibility defect.

Three results are load-bearing. First, the handoff is a \emph{type contract},
not a name-matching heuristic: the backbone is a resnet20 whose classifier has
been replaced by \code{nn.Identity}, so its \code{state\_dict} is by
construction the resnet20 key set minus \code{linear.*}, and
\code{load\_init\_weights} rejects any checkpoint that deviates rather than
silently loading a subset. Second, and the substantive finding: at a
\emph{fixed} total budget, moving compute out of supervised per-expert
finetuning and into unsupervised shared pretraining produces a
\emph{worse} starting population but a \emph{better} final one. Across the
equal-budget family the rank correlation between initial expert accuracy and
final accuracy is $-1.00$; the best equal-budget allocation, $9$ epochs of SSL
plus $0.3$ of finetune, starts at $0.540$ three-class accuracy---the lowest of
the family---and ends at $0.872$, against $0.744 \to 0.843$ for the allocation
that starts strongest. Starting quality is not what the evolution consumes;
shared origin is.

Third, a defect that costs reproducibility rather than correctness. The
backbone checkpoints on disk were produced when an epoch meant one full pass
($97$ updates at batch $512$); the current default is $20$ updates. Re-running
\file{train\_ssl.py} today with unchanged flags yields a \code{backbone\_e9}
that cost $1.84$ full-set passes instead of $8.94$---a factor of $4.85$---under
the same filename. Every figure charges the \emph{existing} artifacts
correctly, and nothing on disk is mislabelled, but the artifacts cannot be
regenerated from the current defaults.
\end{abstract}

\section{What was built}

\subsection{SimSiam on a 256-dimensional backbone}

SimSiam (Chen \& He, 2021) takes two augmented views of one image through a
shared backbone and projection MLP, maps one view's projection to the other's
with a prediction MLP, and stop-gradients the target branch. The loss is the
symmetrised negative cosine similarity,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \tfrac12 \mathcal{D}(p_1, \mathrm{sg}[z_2])
            + \tfrac12 \mathcal{D}(p_2, \mathrm{sg}[z_1]),
\qquad
\mathcal{D}(p,z) = -\frac{p}{\lVert p\rVert}\cdot\frac{z}{\lVert z\rVert}.
\end{equation}
No negatives, no momentum encoder, no large batch---which is why it was chosen
over SimCLR or MoCo for a single-GPU budget.

Four adaptations were required, each forced by the target architecture rather
than chosen:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Feature width.} resnet20$\times$4's average-pool output is
      $w\cdot 64 = 256$-d, not the $2048$ of a full ResNet. The projection
      hidden width is scaled to $512$ (from $2048$) and the prediction
      bottleneck to $128$ (from $512$), preserving SimSiam's roughly $4{:}1$
      bottleneck ratio rather than copying its absolute widths.
\item \textbf{No Gaussian blur.} SimSiam's augmentation stack includes blur,
      which is close to a no-op at $32\times32$; it is dropped. The rest of the
      stack is kept: \code{RandomResizedCrop(32, scale=(0.2,1.0))}, horizontal
      flip, colour jitter $(0.4,0.4,0.4,0.1)$ at $p{=}0.8$, grayscale at
      $p{=}0.2$.
\item \textbf{\code{drop\_last=True}.} SimSiam's projection and prediction MLPs
      are BatchNorm-heavy; a short trailing batch produces unstable statistics.
      This is also why a ``full pass'' is $97$ batches at batch $512$, not
      $97.6$---see Section~\ref{sec:break}.
\item \textbf{Grayscale-to-RGB first.} For 1-channel datasets the conversion
      must precede colour jitter, whose saturation and hue terms are undefined
      on an \code{L} image, and precede the 3-channel normalisation, which
      would otherwise fail to broadcast.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The checkpoint contract}

The key design decision is that \emph{one} SSL run serves \emph{every} budget
point. A backbone is written after every epoch, so \code{backbone\_e3},
\code{backbone\_e6}, \code{backbone\_e9} and \code{backbone\_e50} are all
prefixes of the same optimisation trajectory rather than separate runs. This
makes the SSL axis of the budget grid a within-run comparison, removing
run-to-run variance from it entirely.

Fractional budgets are supported by mid-epoch snapshots:
\code{-{}-half-checkpoint 7.5} saves once epoch~8 has processed half its
batches. The single 50-epoch run
(\file{runs\_ssl/simsiam\_e50\_seed0}) produced $51$ checkpoints in
$16{,}318$\,s ($4.5$\,h) and the SSL loss fell monotonically apart from noise:
$-0.518$ at epoch~1, $-0.723$ at epoch~9, $-0.864$ at epoch~50.

\subsection{The backbone-to-expert handoff}

This is the part most likely to fail silently, so it was made structural. The
backbone is not a bespoke module---it is

\begin{lstlisting}
net = resnet20(w=width, num_classes=10)
net.linear = nn.Identity()
\end{lstlisting}

so its \code{state\_dict} is \emph{exactly} the resnet20 key set minus
\code{linear.*}. \code{load\_init\_weights} then dispatches on the presence of
\code{linear.weight}:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Backbone checkpoint} (no head): loaded with \code{strict=False} so
      the trunk fills in and each expert's classifier stays randomly
      initialised, ready to learn its 3-class subset.
\item \textbf{Full-width checkpoint} (joint baseline): loaded strictly, with the
      head width checked against the dataset's class count---because the whole
      pipeline depends on experts staying in \emph{original} label coordinates,
      never remapped to $0..k{-}1$.
\end{itemize}

\code{strict=False} is the dangerous part of that design, since it will happily
ignore a checkpoint that shares only a few key names. Two explicit guards
convert that into an error:

\begin{lstlisting}
if stray:                 raise ValueError(... unexpected keys not in resnet20 ...)
if non_linear_missing:    raise ValueError(... missing backbone keys ...)
\end{lstlisting}

so the only tolerated missing keys are \code{linear.*} and the only tolerated
outcome is a fully-populated trunk.

\subsection{Fractional per-expert finetuning}

The budget study needs finetune budgets well below one epoch, so
\code{\_finetune\_sub\_epochs} runs a fixed batch count
$\mathrm{round}(S \cdot \mathrm{nb})$ with a per-batch cosine schedule over that
truncated length. It is reached only on the \code{-{}-init-from} path, so the
integer-epoch loop used by the original experiments is untouched. At $S{=}0.1$
on a 3-class subset ($15{,}000$ images, batch $500$, $30$ batches per subset
epoch) each expert sees exactly \textbf{3 batches}.

\subsection{Closed-form head calibration (the $S \to 0$ endpoint)}

To place a strict zero-SGD endpoint on the finetune axis,
\code{calibrate\_closed\_form} fills the classifier by ridge least squares on
frozen backbone features:
\begin{equation}
W^{*} = \arg\min_W \lVert X_{\text{aug}} W - T\rVert^2 + \lambda \lVert W\rVert^2,
\qquad X_{\text{aug}} = [\,X \;\; \mathbf{1}\,],
\end{equation}
solved directly. The backbone is untouched and no gradient step is taken.

One non-obvious detail is recorded in the code and worth repeating, because the
natural implementation fails: \textbf{all} rows of $W$ must be written, not just
the subset's. Non-subset classes have all-zero targets, so ridge drives their
rows to $\approx 0$. If instead the other rows are left at their random
initialisation, those large random rows win the \code{argmax} against the
modestly-scaled closed-form rows---measured subset accuracy $0.003$, i.e.\ below
chance.

\section{The budget ledger}
\label{sec:ledger}

Comparing ``SSL epochs'' against ``per-expert finetune epochs'' requires one
unit. Everything is charged in \textbf{full-set-equivalent epochs}: one unit is
one pass over the $50{,}000$-image CIFAR-10 train split.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item $E$ = SSL epochs. The SSL loader covers the whole train set, so $E$ is
      already in full-set units.
\item $S$ = per-expert finetune sub-epochs. A 3-class subset is
      $15{,}000/50{,}000 = 0.3$ of the full set, and there are $10$ experts, so
      the population's total finetune cost is
      \begin{equation}
      y \;=\; 10 \times 0.3 \times S \;=\; 3S .
      \end{equation}
\item Total population budget before evolution $= E + y$.
\item Evolution adds $20$ epochs per generation ($10$ offspring $\times$ $2$
      full-set epochs), identically for every arm, so it never differentiates
      the runs.
\end{itemize}

Worked example for \code{exp2d}: $E{=}9$, $S{=}0.1$, so each expert trains on
$3$ batches of $500$; ten experts consume $15{,}000$ images $=0.3$ epochs; total
$9.3$. This is the ledger the figure labels use (``SSL 9ep + 0.3ep'').

\code{updates\_for} is what makes this hold under any epoch definition: it
charges a subset loader its \emph{proportional} share of
\code{-{}-updates-per-epoch}, so $y = 3S$ regardless of how many updates an
epoch is declared to be.

\section{Verification}

\subsection{Load-path tests}

\file{tools/check\_init\_backbone.py} runs four checks with no GPU and no
training, all passing:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item A saved SimSiam backbone auto-detects to the backbone-only path; trunk
      weights match the checkpoint bit-for-bit; the classifier is left at its
      random initialisation.
\item A full 10-wide checkpoint auto-detects to the strict path and its head
      \emph{is} loaded.
\item \code{-{}-init-backbone-only} forced on a full checkpoint ignores that
      checkpoint's head and keeps the random one.
\item A fractional finetune runs exactly the expected number of batches.
\end{enumerate}

Tests 1--3 pin the dispatch logic; test 4 pins the ledger.

\subsection{Linear probe}

A weak evolution result at some $E$ could be a weak-backbone problem or an
evolution problem. The probe separates them: freeze the backbone (in
\code{eval()}, so its BatchNorm statistics cannot move), train one linear head
for one epoch on labelled CIFAR-10, record test accuracy. It deliberately uses
\code{load\_init\_weights(..., backbone\_only=True)}---the same loader
\code{exp2} uses---so a probe that loads is itself evidence the experiment's
init path loads.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
$E$ & 1 & 3 & 6 & 7.5 & 10 & 20 & 30 & 50 \\
\midrule
probe acc & $0.214$ & $0.188$ & $0.316$ & $0.288$ & $0.346$ & $0.367$ &
$0.354$ & $\mathbf{0.401}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The trend $0.21 \to 0.40$ is monotone up; two local dips are not, and the
run's own \code{probe.json} records why they were accepted rather than chased:
a 1-epoch probe has a few points of run-to-run variance, and $E{=}7.5$ is a
mid-epoch snapshot whose BatchNorm running statistics were captured mid-sweep.
What the probe is used for is the \emph{ordering and gaps} across checkpoints,
and those hold.

Note the absolute level: $0.40$ after $50$ epochs is a weak linear probe by SSL
standards. That is expected at this budget---the point of the exercise is a
cheap shared origin, not a strong representation---but it should be stated
plainly rather than implied away.

\subsection{Does the backbone help at all?}

Yes, and the comparison is budget-matched. \code{half} is SESiL's original
design---ten experts pretrained independently and supervised, $9$ epochs of
population budget, no shared origin---and reaches $0.804$. \code{exp2d} spends
$9.3$ and reaches $0.872$: $+0.068$ for a $3\%$ larger budget. The joint-training
upper reference (one network, all labels, $520$ epochs) is $0.957$.

\subsection{At equal budget, unsupervised beats supervised}

The four allocations at total $\approx 9$ are the controlled comparison, and
they are perfectly ordered---in \emph{both} directions:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
& \textbf{$E$ (SSL)} & \textbf{init 3-class} & \textbf{final} \\
\midrule
\code{exp2a} & $3$   & $0.744$ & $0.843$ \\
\code{exp2b} & $6$   & $0.732$ & $0.854$ \\
\code{exp2c} & $7.5$ & $0.691$ & $0.858$ \\
\code{exp2d} & $9$   & $0.540$ & $0.872$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Spearman $\rho(E, \text{final}) = +1.00$ and
$\rho(\text{init}, \text{final}) = -1.00$ over this family. \textbf{The
allocation that produces the worst starting population produces the best final
one}, and the effect is not marginal: $0.540 \to 0.872$ against
$0.744 \to 0.843$.

The natural reading is that what evolution consumes is not per-expert skill but
\emph{mergeability}. Heavy supervised finetuning buys accuracy by driving each
expert away from the common origin; the merge operator then has to reconcile
ten networks that have drifted apart. Unsupervised shared pretraining buys the
opposite: weak experts that all still sit near one point in weight space. Over
25 generations of merging, the second is worth more. This is consistent with
the task-vector norms measured in the companion report, where merge quality
degrades as parents separate---but it is an interpretation, not something this
budget study isolates on its own.

itself moves down as the core gets stronger---but with one seed and one point
below the floor, that is a conjecture, not a result.

\section{Defects and risks}

\subsection{The epoch-definition break}
\label{sec:break}

This is the one item that should block a reproduction attempt.

The SSL run's \code{history.json} records its full configuration, and that
record contains \textbf{no} \code{updates\_per\_epoch} field---the run predates
the commit that introduced it. It therefore used the original definition, where
an epoch is one pass over the loader. At batch $512$ with
\code{drop\_last=True} that is $\lfloor 50000/512 \rfloor = 97$ updates.

\code{SSLConfig.updates\_per\_epoch} now defaults to $20$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
& \textbf{updates/epoch} & \textbf{images in \code{backbone\_e9}} &
\textbf{full-set passes} \\
\midrule
as produced (on disk) & $97$ & $446{,}976$ & $8.94$ \\
if regenerated today & $20$ & $92{,}160$ & $1.84$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent A factor of $4.85$, under an unchanged filename and an unchanged
command line. Corroborating evidence for the $97$: epoch~1 took $226.5$\,s,
which at $20$ updates would imply $11$\,s per SimSiam step on this hardware.

What this does and does not mean:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{No published number is wrong.} The checkpoints on disk really did
      cost $\approx 9$ full-set epochs, and Section~\ref{sec:ledger} charges
      them as $9$. The figures are correct about the artifacts they plot.
\item \textbf{The artifacts are not reproducible from current defaults.}
      Anyone re-running \file{runs\_ssl/launch\_ssl.sh} gets backbones roughly
      five times cheaper than the ones every \code{exp2} result was built on.
\item \textbf{The fix is to pin the flag, not to change the default.} The
      new default is the better convention---it makes an epoch mean the same
      number of gradient steps in the SSL, pretrain and mutation stages, and it
      removes the batch-size wrinkle whereby a ``full pass'' is $97$ updates
      rather than a round number. Reproduction should pass
      \code{-{}-updates-per-epoch 0} explicitly.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\code{exp2e} did not finish}

The closed-form calibration path is implemented and its subtle failure mode is
fixed and documented (Section~2.5), but
\file{runs\_exp2e/seed0/permute/csv} is empty and no \file{run.json} was
written: the population was built and the evolution never produced a
generation. The strict $S \to 0$ endpoint of the finetune axis is therefore
\textbf{unmeasured}. \code{exp2i} ($y{=}0.3$, $0.797$) is the cheapest finetune
point that actually has a result.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{One seed.} Every number here is seed~0. A multi-seed campaign was
      queued and cancelled. The equal-budget ordering is perfectly monotone
      across four points, which is suggestive, but four points at one seed
      cannot separate a real ordering from a lucky one.
\item \textbf{The SSL axis is a within-run comparison.} $E{=}3,6,7.5,9,50$ are
      prefixes of a single trajectory. This is a feature---it removes
      run-to-run variance from that axis---but it also means the $E$ sweep
      carries \emph{no} independent replication at all, and any pathology of
      that one run propagates to every point.
\item \textbf{The probe is relative, not absolute.} One epoch of linear probing
      under-reads asymptotic probe accuracy. It is valid for ordering
      checkpoints and was only used for that.
\item \textbf{The mergeability interpretation is not isolated.} The claim that
      shared origin beats per-expert skill \emph{because} it keeps parents
      mergeable is consistent with the evidence but is not tested here; the
      budget study varies allocation, not distance between parents. A direct
      test would hold allocation fixed and vary only how far the experts are
      allowed to drift.
\item \textbf{The finetune floor is one point.} \code{exp2d} wins from below the
      apparent floor established at $E{=}6$. Whether the floor genuinely moves
      with core strength needs the $E{=}9$ finetune sweep, which was not run.
\item \textbf{Only CIFAR-10, only resnet20$\times$4, only 10 agents of 3
      classes.} The dataset flag added later makes other datasets runnable, but
      no other dataset has been run through this stage.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reproduction}
